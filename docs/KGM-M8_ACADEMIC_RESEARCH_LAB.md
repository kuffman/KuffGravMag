# KGM-M8 Academic and Research Laboratory

The Academic & Research Laboratory provides repeatable synthetic profiles for classroom demonstrations and method experiments. It is available from the desktop Studio workflow and the Streamlit workspace. Every export identifies itself as synthetic, records the model parameters and random seed, and includes the model equation and limitations.

## Included source models

All profiles use local Cartesian coordinates: X is distance along the survey line, Z is positive down, and Y is the infinite strike direction. Stations are placed along a synthetic flat profile. Source centers and depths are specified in metres.

### Gravity

For a homogeneous sphere with radius `R`, density contrast `Δρ`, centre `(x_c,z_c)`, and volume `V=4πR³/3`, the external vertical attraction is

`g_z = G Δρ V (z_c-z) / ((x-x_c)²+(z-z_c)²)^(3/2)`.

Inside the sphere, the uniform-sphere enclosed-mass solution is used:

`g_z = (4πGΔρ/3)(z_c-z)`.

For an infinite circular cylinder with cross-sectional area `A=πR²`, the external vertical attraction is

`g_z = 2GΔρ A (z_c-z) / ((x-x_c)²+(z-z_c)²)`.

Inside the cylinder, `g_z = 2πGΔρ(z_c-z)`. The vertical dyke uses the existing exact infinite-strike rectangular polygon kernel. Gravity is reported in mGal.

### Magnetics

Induced magnetization is combined with a declared remanent vector:

`M = κ(F/μ₀)u_F + J_r u_r`.

For sphere displacement vector `r` from its centre and moment `m=VM`, the outside field is `B=(μ₀/4π)[3r(m·r)/|r|⁵-m/|r|³]`; inside, `B=2μ₀M/3`. For an infinite cylinder, let `r⊥=(x-x_c,z-z_c)` and `M⊥=(M_x,M_z)`. Outside, `H=(R²/2)[2r⊥(M⊥·r⊥)/|r⊥|⁴-M⊥/|r⊥|²]` and `B=μ₀H`; inside, `H=-M⊥/2` and `B=μ₀(H+M⊥)=μ₀M⊥/2`. The dyke uses the existing exact infinite-strike polygon boundary-charge kernel. The total-field anomaly is the first-order projection of the modeled field onto the ambient field direction. Components and anomalies are in nT.

The sphere and cylinder models describe uniformly magnetized bodies; they do not solve the self-demagnetization feedback that would alter susceptibility-induced magnetization. The cylinder and dyke have no strike ends. A magnetization component parallel to infinite strike therefore contributes no field in these 2-D models.

## Controlled experiments and inversion

Both interfaces let the learner choose the source, field, dimensions, density or magnetic properties, ambient field, profile azimuth, station count, noise standard deviation, and random seed. Noise is independent, zero-mean Gaussian generated by NumPy's `default_rng(seed)`. Repeating an experiment with the same inputs and seed reproduces the same profile.

For a sphere gravity profile, the lab fits density contrast and centre depth with bounded weighted least squares. It reports unweighted and weighted RMS, numerical Jacobian rank, a condition-based local identifiability flag, and local linear standard errors when the Jacobian has full rank and residual degrees of freedom are available. A one-station density-depth fit is intentionally shown as non-unique and does not report standard errors. Different depth-density models are also compared: they can match a single station while leaving different full-profile residuals.

The CSV export contains X, observation elevation, noise-free model, generated noise, and noisy observation columns. Its first comment line records all settings, units, equation, seed, synthetic-data flag, and limitations. Seeded examples are reproducible; the numerical noise sample is not intended as a model of all field-survey error sources.

## Ghana-relevant synthetic context

The interface and teaching examples use a local profile frame with a declared classroom coordinate anchor near 3°W, 6°N (EPSG:32630; easting 500,000 m, northing 663,204.567 m). It is only a coordinate reference for an illustrative Ghana-region exercise. The anomalies, geometry, densities, susceptibilities, and magnetization are generated by the model and are not Ghana field observations or claims about local geology. This synthetic context is included in the CSV metadata. Students should replace the assumptions with properly documented field data only in a separate, validated interpretation workflow.

## Validation and limits

`tests/test_academic_lab.py` compares sphere gravity with its external point-mass solution, checks infinite-cylinder and dyke gravity against independent cross-section area quadrature, checks sphere magnetics against the dipole equation and cylinder magnetics against independent boundary-charge quadrature, and covers dyke magnetics, symmetry, interior limits, seeded export, inversion recovery, uncertainty scaling, non-uniqueness, and invalid geometries. Desktop offscreen coverage checks that the Studio action opens the experiment interface and that seeded sphere and magnetic-cylinder experiments run. The macOS smoke exercises the same desktop views, and the web/PDF smoke checks that the Streamlit lab panel shows its equation and seeded CSV action.

This increment does not include finite-length cylinder end effects, arbitrary 3-D geological surfaces, magnetic parameter inversion, self-demagnetization inversion, Bayesian posterior sampling, geological priors, real-time field acquisition, or claims of Ghana site validation. The inversion lesson currently fits density contrast and depth for a spherical gravity source only. Its uncertainty remains a local linear approximation conditional on the model and supplied noise; it is not total geological uncertainty.
